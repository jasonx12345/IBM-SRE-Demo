# What this tool cannot see

All toil figures are estimates. This dashboard supports a conversation about what to improve; it cannot measure productivity, establish actual labor costs, or decide whether an alert is safe to remove.

- **Work outside the alerting system is invisible.** Support requests, chat investigations, maintenance, undocumented checks and incident follow-ups are absent. A quiet alert history does not mean a team has little toil.
- **Context-switching cost is not captured.** Pages can wake a responder or interrupt concentration even when no remediation follows. A zero manual-toil estimate for CPU noise does not mean that noise is free.
- **Several alerts from one incident may double-count.** The incident ID contributes to the incident rate, but the model deliberately does not deduplicate or allocate responder time across correlated alerts. The two synthetic storms make volume spikes visible; they do not resolve this attribution problem.
- **Acknowledge-to-resolve time can overstate hands-on work.** Someone can acknowledge an alert, wait for an external dependency or walk away, and resolve it later. The manual flag improves the proxy but does not measure active minutes.
- **Parallel responders are only approximated.** The model multiplies a median duration by a mean responder count. It assumes equal engagement and may count waiting or uneven participation as work. A zero responder count on a manual event is floored to one in the formula.
- **Thresholds are judgement calls, not derived from data.** The configured rates and sample counts are defensible starting points, not learned cutoffs or statistically validated boundaries. The fixed classification order can suppress a later label, and changing the observation window can change eligibility.
- **Rare, high-consequence signals can resemble noise.** A low incident-link rate may reflect poor incident declaration rather than low value. Deletion candidates require review of service risk, coverage and escalation paths before any operational change.
- **Repeated runbooks are not proof of safe automation.** The same named action might require judgement, different parameters, permission checks, or have unpredictable side effects. The repeat rate is a proxy for repeatability, not an automation success probability.
- **Missing data biases the estimate.** Unknown runbooks depress repeat rate. Missing durations are omitted from the median and represented by remaining observations; if none remain, the type has an unknown estimate and is excluded from totals. Incorrect manual flags or responder counts distort the result.
- **Annualization assumes the observed period is representative.** Six months may miss seasonal load, release spikes, growth, changing alert definitions, and resolved reliability problems. The model does not extrapolate trends or provide a confidence interval.
- **Gross savings are not net return.** Default savings assume all modeled work disappears, including non-modal resolutions. Engineering/build time, ongoing maintenance, human review and new failure modes are excluded. The configured savings fractions make partial coverage explicit but do not validate it.
- **Alert names can hide different work.** The aggregation combines services sharing the same alert name, which may conceal service-specific remediation or different teams' practices.
- **Time rules are simplified.** After hours means outside 09:00–17:00 in one configured IANA timezone every day. Holidays, weekends, rotating schedules and geographically distributed responders are not modeled.
- **The data is synthetic.** The generator was designed to exercise these archetypes and produce reviewable results. It cannot establish how accurate these heuristics would be on a real team's data, and no real PagerDuty or Alertmanager integration is included.
- **This service is sized for the project dataset.** A cold analysis loads all events into application memory, then caches the result for 15 seconds. Millions of events need incremental ingestion, database-side aggregation and a more durable analysis cache.
- **Operational metrics have boundaries too.** Ingestion measurements survive app restarts because they are persisted, but a total database failure can prevent recording a failed ingestion. App latency does not include browser rendering or network time to the caller. Request counters reset when a process restarts, which Prometheus rates handle.

Interpret every recommendation with its visible reason, samples, runbook breakdown and monthly trend. Validate the underlying data and discuss the candidate with its service owner before acting.
