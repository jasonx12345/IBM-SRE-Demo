THE PROJECT IN BRIEF

Checks whether the warnings IBM Research expected during its test failures actually showed up.

What this is: IBM Research deliberately caused failures in test systems and published the warnings it expected. I built a report that checks those expectations against the warnings recorded during each test. It shows what appeared, what was not seen, and what the saved data cannot tell us.

Why it matters for SRE: Alerts that fail to appear when they should can leave outages unnoticed. This checks whether the expected warnings showed up in these tests.

How it works

  • IBM's public ITBench-Lite data declares which alerts should fire in each of 35 fault experiments.
  • I wrote the matching logic that checks the alert snapshots against those declarations and labels each expected alert observed, not observed, or unknown.
  • The second, local lab replays 37 alert episodes from two experiments to test whether removing noisy alerts together creates blind spots.

Key findings

  • 35 public experiments audited.
  • The retirement lab uses 37 alert episodes normalized from 82 snapshots.
  • Removing both frontend-proxy warning scopes removes six episodes and leaves the overload experiment with no ground-truth-linked warning.
  • Keeping the proxy error-rate scope repairs both outcomes.

This is a test-quality report on public IBM Research data, not IBM production telemetry. It makes no novelty claim.
